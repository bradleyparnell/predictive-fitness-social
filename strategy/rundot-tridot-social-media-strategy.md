### 1.3 What TriDot Is Currently Doing

**Strengths vs. RunDot:** Better content variety, stronger institutional credibility, larger community, richer ambassador ecosystem (Legends: Mark Allen 6X Ironman Champ, Craig Alexander 3X Ironman Champ, Mirinda Carfrae 3X Ironman Champ, Michellie Jones Olympic Medalist, Tim O'Donnell ITU World Champ).

---

### 1.4 The Gap: What Neither Brand Is Doing Well

1. **UGC is nearly invisible** — Users are posting about these apps organically, but almost none of it is being reshared or amplified. This is a massive missed opportunity for social proof.
2. **No recurring content series** — Audiences build habits around predictable series. Neither brand has an anchor format (e.g., "Monday Motivation," "Science Saturday," "Athlete of the Week").
3. **Low scroll-stop power** — Too many posts open with a generic visual or a text-forward slide. Top-performing fitness content opens with a provocation, a stat, or a face.
4. **Weak CTA clarity** — "Try free at RunDot.com" doesn't give a friction-free path to download. App store links, QR prompts embedded in Reels end cards, and Instagram bio link-in-bio optimization are underused.
5. **Underusing world-class ambassadors** — Meb Keflezighi is on the RunDot bio, yet there's no rich ambassador storytelling series. TriDot's Legends roster (Mark Allen et al.) are largely invisible on the feed.
6. **Before/after transformation content is absent** — This is the highest-converting content type in fitness apps and it's essentially not used.
7. **Reels output is inconsistent** — The Instagram algorithm heavily favors Reels. Neither brand is posting Reels at scale with consistent quality.
8. **Facebook groups are underutilized** — TriDot has an "I AM TriDot – Chicago" group. Neither brand has a centralized, actively managed flagship Facebook community group tied to the main page.
9. **Cross-brand synergy missing** — RunDot and TriDot share the same FitLogic engine. Cross-promotional content ("RunDot for your run legs, TriDot for your whole tri") is essentially non-existent.

## SECTION 2: STRATEGIC FRAMEWORK

### 2.1 The Four E's — Every Post Serves One Primary Purpose

Every piece of content must map to one of these four pillars:

| Pillar | Purpose | Share of Content |
|---|---|---|
| 🎭 **Entertain** | Stop the scroll. Build community. Go shareable. | 25% |
| 📚 **Educate** | Build authority. Answer real training questions. Save-worthy. | 30% |
| 💡 **Enlighten** | Shift beliefs. Inspire emotion. Challenge myths. | 25% |
| 📲 **Call to Action** | Drive app downloads and free trials. | 20% |

---

### 2.2 Brand Voice Guidelines

**RunDot voice:** Encouraging, accessible, modern. Speaks to the everyday runner who wants to get smarter — not just harder. Uses "you" language heavily. Doesn't talk down. Celebrates consistency as much as speed.

**TriDot voice:** Science-confident, community-focused, elite-by-science-not-attitude. Speaks to triathletes who take their sport seriously but also have full lives. Deep expertise worn lightly. Celebrates finishing and improvement, not just podiums.

---

### 2.3 Audience Personas

**RunDot primary personas:**
1. **The Comeback Runner** — 35–55, previously injured or returning after a break, wants to run smart and stay healthy
2. **The Busy Achiever** — 28–45, time-poor, wants to train efficiently without sacrificing results or family time
3. **The First-Timer** — Training for a first 5K, 10K, or half marathon, overwhelmed by generic plans
4. **The Experienced Optimizer** — Has run many races, wants real data-driven personalization

**TriDot primary personas:**
1. **The Aspiring Ironman** — Training for their first 70.3 or full Ironman, needs structured multi-sport guidance
2. **The Experienced Triathlete** — Wants PR improvement and smarter training, not more volume
3. **The Community Seeker** — Motivated by the group experience, accountability, and the TriDot culture
4. **The Data Geek** — Loves the science, NTS™, and optimization — wants to understand the "why"

## SECTION 3: CONTENT PILLARS & SERIES

### 3.1 RunDot Content Series (Recurring Formats)

These named series should be consistent in visual style, cadence, and format:

---

#### 🏃 **"Train Less. Run More."** *(Monday — Enlighten)*
**Format:** Single-image graphic or short Reel (15–30 sec)
**Concept:** Debunk a running myth. Challenge the "no pain no gain" mentality. Align with the brand's core message that smarter training beats harder training.
**Examples:**
- "Running more miles doesn't make you faster. Here's what does."
- "You don't need to feel destroyed after a run. That's a myth."
- "Why easy runs feel embarrassingly slow — and why that's the point."

---

#### 🤝 **"Your Run, Your Story"** *(Wednesday — UGC/Entertain)*
**Format:** Repurposed UGC photo + quote overlay, OR a short video testimonial
**Concept:** Feature a real RunDot athlete's story, milestone, or review. Always tag them. Always credit them. Always include their result or achievement.
**Examples:**
- Kathy, 63, running pain-free and PR'ing
- J. Richards: "11-minute personal best"
- Vanessa Coronado: "After 30 years, I finally understand heart rate zones"

---

#### 🧠 **"Run Smart" Tips** *(Thursday — Educate)*
**Format:** 5–10-slide educational carousel
**Concept:** Teach one specific training concept, science fact, or app feature per carousel. Highly saveable. Use clear headers, minimal text per slide, and a CTA on the final slide.
**Examples:**
- "Heart Rate Zones, Explained Simply"
- "What Is VO2 Max and Why Runners Should Care"
- "Why RunDot Adjusts Your Pace Based on Heat and Humidity"
- "Cruise Intervals: The Workout You're Probably Skipping"

---

#### 🎬 **"Meb Moments"** *(Bi-weekly — Entertain/Enlighten)*
**Format:** Short video clip, quote graphic, or behind-the-scenes Reel featuring Meb Keflezighi
**Concept:** Meb is a once-in-a-generation running figure — and he's on the platform. His voice lends credibility to every type of content: motivational, technical, community. This series should run every other week minimum.
**Examples:**
- Meb sharing one coaching tip he uses with his RunDot athletes
- Meb reacting to a RunDot athlete milestone
- Meb Q&A: "What do you wish runners understood about base training?"

---

#### 🗓️ **"Race Week Countdown"** *(Seasonal/Event-tied — CTA)*
**Format:** Carousels, short Reels, and Stories tied to partner race events
**Concept:** Leading up to LA Marathon, SF Marathon, Rose Bowl Half, etc. — build hype with training tips, athlete shoutouts, and direct app download CTAs for race registrants.
**Examples:**
- "LA Marathon is in 4 weeks. Here's exactly what RunDot prescribes."
- "Are you running the SF Marathon? Drop a 🏃 below."
- "Race day prediction tool — how close does RunDot get?"

---

#### 📲 **"Download. Try. Prove It."** *(Friday — CTA)*
**Format:** Short Reel (15–30 sec) or single image
**Concept:** Direct, confident app download push. Use social proof (star ratings, number of athletes, real testimonials) and a crystal-clear CTA. Rotate between "try free" offer, specific persona targeting, and event hooks.
**Examples:**
- "14-day free trial. No credit card. Just run." + app store link
- "4.8 stars. 11-minute PRs. One app." + download link
- "Official training app of the LA Marathon — try it free" + link

## SECTION 5: USER-GENERATED CONTENT (UGC) STRATEGY

### 5.1 How to Source UGC

1. **Hashtag monitoring:** Track #RunDot, #RunDotTraining, #TriDot, #TriDotTraining, #IAMTriDot daily. Screenshot and save any athlete posts with results, race photos, or testimonials.

2. **Stories resharing:** Every time a user tags the brand in a Story, reshare it immediately to the brand's own Story with a "🔥 Love this!" reaction.

3. **DM outreach:** When an athlete posts a particularly compelling milestone (PR, first race, comeback story), DM them personally, congratulate them, and ask permission to feature them on the feed.

4. **In-app prompts:** Add a UGC prompt inside the app itself post-workout: "Share your run? Tag @rundottraining for a chance to be featured."

5. **Community Facebook groups:** Monitor the TriDot community groups (e.g., I AM TriDot – Chicago) for compelling posts. Request permission to cross-post to the main page.

6. **Post-race window:** After every partner race (LA Marathon, SF Marathon, SuperTri events, etc.), actively solicit athlete content via Stories polls and post-race hashtags.

---

### 5.2 UGC Content Types to Prioritize

| Type | Why It Works |
|---|---|
| **Finish line photos** | Emotional, high-shareable, validates the app's impact |
| **PR announcements** | Quantifiable proof. "11-minute PR" is more compelling than any ad |
| **Before/after training stories** | Highest-converting content type in fitness |
| **App screenshot shares** | Shows the product in real use; builds desire |
| **Older athlete milestones** | Kathy at 63 running injury-free = massive relatability for a huge demographic |
| **First-time finishers** | Emotional arc, highly shareable, broadens the audience beyond elites |

---

### 5.3 UGC Republishing Best Practices

- Always tag the original athlete
- Always ask permission before republishing to the feed (Stories resharing is generally accepted)
- Add minimal branded overlay — let the athlete content breathe
- Write captions in the athlete's voice, not corporate voice
- For high-performing UGC, boost it as a paid ad (with permission)

## SECTION 6: PROFESSIONALLY DESIGNED CONTENT GUIDELINES

### 6.1 Visual Identity Standards

**RunDot:**
- Primary palette: RunDot brand colors (black, white, accent green/teal from website)
- Typography: Clean, modern sans-serif — bold headers, light body
- Photography style: Real athletes in real conditions — outdoors, sweaty, authentic. Avoid stock-photo perfection.
- Motion: Reels should feel like premium fitness brand content — tight edits, great music, clear text overlays
- App UI screenshots: Use in every 3rd–4th post to normalize the product in athletes' minds

**TriDot:**
- Primary palette: TriDot blue, white, black
- Typography: Consistent across all templates — build a Canva/Figma system
- Photography style: Multi-sport energy — open water, cycling on roads, running finish lines. Group shots from events alongside solo athlete content.
- Science graphics: Clean data visualizations when explaining NTS™, training zones, etc. — make science visual and accessible
- App UI screenshots: Use discipline-specific screens (swim complete, bike schedule, run workout)

---

### 6.2 Reel Production Standards

**Length:** 15–60 seconds for most content. 60–90 seconds max for educational content.

**Structure:**
- Seconds 0–3: **Hook** — a provocative statement, surprising stat, or relatable scenario ("I ran fewer miles this month than ever. Here's why my time went down.")
- Seconds 3–45: **Payload** — deliver the education, story, or entertainment
- Seconds 45–60: **CTA** — simple, direct, one action ("Try RunDot free. Link in bio.")

**Music:** Use trending audio when available. Keep voiceover and captions for accessibility.

**Captions:** Always add captions. 85% of Reels are watched without sound.

**End card:** Every Reel should end with a branded frame: logo + one CTA + app store badge.

---

### 6.3 Carousel Design Standards

- **Slide 1:** Hook headline only — bold, large, provocative. Think "magazine cover."
- **Slides 2–9:** One concept per slide. Keep text minimal. Use icons, visuals, or data where possible.
- **Last slide:** Always a CTA — "Try RunDot free. Download the app." with a QR code or direct link call-out.
- **Swipe prompt:** Include "Swipe for tip #2 →" on slide 1 to drive engagement rate.
- Carousels get repeat impressions when users save and share — optimize for saves, not just likes.

## SECTION 7: CALL TO ACTION — APP DOWNLOAD STRATEGY

### 7.1 CTA Hierarchy

**Primary CTA:** Download the app (App Store / Google Play)
**Secondary CTA:** Start free 14-day trial at RunDot.com / TriDot.com
**Tertiary CTA:** Take the personalization quiz / Follow for more

---

### 7.2 CTA Best Practices

1. **Bio optimization:** Both Instagram bios should have one link — a smart link page (Linktree or similar) with: Download App (iOS), Download App (Android), Free Trial, Website. Update the featured link during race season to match campaign.

2. **Stories CTA stickers:** Every Friday Stories sequence should end with a Link sticker to the app download page. Use a countdown sticker in the week before a race event.

3. **Every Reel end card:** App store badge + QR code graphic as the final frame.

4. **Caption structure:** Educational or entertaining caption body, then a hard line break, then: "⬇️ 14 days free. No credit card. Download the RunDot app — link in bio."

5. **Comment-seeding:** Post the app download link in the first comment of every CTA post to avoid algorithmic suppression of link-in-caption posts.

6. **Facebook-specific CTAs:** Facebook allows native CTA buttons on posts and pages. Set the page button to "Download App." Pin a post linking to the free trial at the top of the Facebook page at all times.

---

## SECTION 8: CROSS-BRAND SYNERGY (RunDot ↔ TriDot)

### 8.1 The Opportunity

RunDot and TriDot are siblings powered by the same FitLogic AI engine. This is an underutilized growth lever. Runners become triathletes. Triathletes want run performance. The brands should feed each other's funnels.

### 8.2 Cross-Promotion Tactics

1. **Mutual shoutouts:** TriDot posts about its run leg — tag RunDot. RunDot posts about runners who do triathlons — tag TriDot.
2. **"From RunDot to TriDot" athlete story series:** Feature athletes who started as RunDot users and graduated to full triathlon training with TriDot.
3. **Shared science content:** FitLogic explainer content can live on both feeds with slight variations — amplifies reach, reinforces the shared brand story.
4. **Follow prompts:** Every RunDot post footer: "Training for a tri? Follow @tridottraining." And vice versa.
